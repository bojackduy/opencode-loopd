# Narrower goals explain more than dashboards

**PrevalentWare’s goal-plugin does a narrower job—keep the current session pursuing a goal—and already has a TUI. Loopd is not an exact duplicate.** Its separate workers, acceptance checks, scheduling, command control, and file coordination serve a broader delegation workflow. Goal-plugin has substantially greater public attention and download activity, but those measures neither count users nor prove architectural superiority. Its earlier launch and external distribution are documented; easier onboarding and reinforcing social proof are plausible explanations, not established causes. Our response should be sharper scope and dependable checked delegation, not another dashboard feature. We also need to acknowledge that we overbuilt and kept patching a critical path whose complexity has hurt trust in the incidents reported here.

## The footprint gap is real; its cause is unproven

At the **October 18, 2026** tool-observed snapshot, goal-plugin had **488 stars versus loopd’s 2**, **13 GitHub-listed contributors versus 1**, **16,240 versus 2,970 monthly npm downloads**, and **2,529 versus 639 weekly downloads**. The returned download windows were September 17–October 16 and October 10–16 respectively. That is roughly 5.5× monthly downloads, not 244× users. Stars measure attention; downloads include upgrades, reinstalls, and automation. Neither establishes unique or active users. ([GitHub measurements](../research_notes/Goal%20plugin%20comparison/adoption.md), [goal downloads](https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-month/@prevalentware/opencode-goal-plugin), [loopd downloads](https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-month/@bojackduy/opencode-loopd))

Goal-plugin’s repository dates to **May 5**, loopd’s to **August 21**; their npm packages were **165 versus 57 completed days old**. PrevalentWare also has independently maintained Nix packaging and explicit third-party usage reports. Loopd has outside mentions and an installation report too: “we never promoted it” is inaccurate. Earlier exposure and distribution are evidence; lower conceptual burden, social proof, and contributor network effects remain hypotheses. Importantly, the official OpenCode ecosystem entry points to **a different same-named goal plugin**, not prevalentWare. ([Registry chronology and distribution evidence](../research_notes/Goal%20plugin%20comparison/adoption.md#2-what-concrete-public-discoverability-and-distribution-evidence-exists), [ecosystem identity correction](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/issues/49456))

## Goal-plugin concentrates engineering on current-session continuation

Its natural unit is **one current goal per session**, with multiple sessions represented in shared state. Creation persists the goal; continuation prompts return to that same session and remembered agent. Legacy idle feedback and v2 execution-success events drive continuation, with native-retry suppression, task deferral, and host-wait integration. This preserves conversational context rather than provisioning an isolated goal worker. Its actual sidebar displays objective, status, elapsed time, tokens, continuations, and checkpoints, alongside lifecycle controls. Calling it “no TUI” or a tiny repeated-prompt toy would be wrong. ([Source-audited execution findings](../research_notes/Goal%20plugin%20comparison/architecture.md#1-what-architecture-and-execution-model-does-the-competitor-actually-implement), [TUI implementation](https://github.com/prevalentWare/opencode-goal-plugin/blob/9c52be63da06829d55e3e0b7a6580223312765b1/src/tui.ts#L536-L628))

Persistence uses JSON, an **in-process mutation queue**, exclusive temporary files, fsync, and atomic rename. Continuation reservations, epochs, attempt IDs, rollback, and post-await checks address lifecycle races; budgets and error classification bound recovery. This is serious reliability work. However, an in-process queue is not cross-process serialization: overlapping read-modify-write operations remain a structural lost-update risk, **not a reproduced or exploited incident**. Durable records also do not establish an always-running external scheduler. ([Persistence implementation](https://github.com/prevalentWare/opencode-goal-plugin/blob/9c52be63da06829d55e3e0b7a6580223312765b1/src/state.ts#L486-L590))

Publicly inspectable tests assert continuation, ownership, waiting, and atomic-write behavior. CI defines quality checks and a real-host v2 fixture smoke journey—stronger evidence than test filenames alone. **We did not execute their tests or conduct live comparative benchmarks**; configured coverage does not establish a green current run, bug-free behavior, superior speed, or fewer defects. ([Host smoke assertions](https://github.com/prevalentWare/opencode-goal-plugin/blob/9c52be63da06829d55e3e0b7a6580223312765b1/scripts/smoke-v2-lifecycle.ts#L138-L184))

## Loopd’s stronger contract also creates more failure boundaries

Loopd documents **separate child workers**, host-native v2 execution supervision, host-run acceptance checks, background tools/process control, scheduling, scoped file coordination, and a supervisory TUI. OpenCode owns execution and queued delivery; loopd adds the objective and acceptance contract. Native v2 execution success does **not** already verify our goal. These distinctions matter when the owner wants to keep chatting while checked work proceeds elsewhere. They also introduce more lifecycle, reservation, compatibility, and recovery boundaries. ([Native supervision](../docs/v2-supervisor.md#L3-L68), [scope contracts](../docs/file-scope-ownership.md#L3-L63), [documented product comparison](../research_notes/Goal%20plugin%20comparison/product.md))

The sharpest verification difference is **model-provided evidence versus host-run acceptance**. Goal-plugin enforces plan order, task completion, and nonempty evidence/verification text; its completion path does not independently execute acceptance commands. Loopd’s configured checks supply an external executable gate. That is stronger for what those checks actually cover, not absolute proof of objective truth; inadequate checks and force-completion bypasses still matter. ([Goal-plugin completion gate](https://github.com/prevalentWare/opencode-goal-plugin/blob/9c52be63da06829d55e3e0b7a6580223312765b1/src/state.ts#L1108-L1147), [loopd check boundaries](../docs/file-scope-ownership.md#L42-L89))

**For “finish this task in this chat,” goal-plugin is the more focused fit. For checked background delegation, loopd has the more relevant contract.** Neither conclusion proves better runtime reliability. Our reported stalls, false nudges, read-blocking locks, and delivery mismatches show complexity hurting the trusted critical path here; they are not public comparative quality statistics or proof every issue persists in 1.14.2. Blaming the gap only on marketing would evade that lesson. ([Incident scope and limitations](../research_notes/Goal%20plugin%20comparison/product.md#1-what-is-each-product-for-and-does-its-tui-solve-the-target-users-need))

## Conclusion

Prioritize **checked background delegation on native v2**. Make the first success a minimal child task that progresses while the owner remains usable, passes meaningful checks, and delivers its result. Gate releases on real-host contract journeys covering retries, busy delivery, restart, cancellation, scope release, and owner notification. Publish precise stop/resume and queue policies. Tier scheduling, process terminals, and advanced coordination behind that dependable core; treat the TUI as visibility, not a moat.

Measure first checked success and lifecycle failures before expanding scope. Those measurements can establish whether simplification improves trust; stars cannot. Separately, the notes confirm **1.14.2 is publicly available** from npm, resolving the earlier pending availability question without explaining the historical 404. ([Public version verification](https://registry.npmjs.org/@bojackduy%2fopencode-loopd/1.14.2))
